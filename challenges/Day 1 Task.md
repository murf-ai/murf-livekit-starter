# Day 1 – Get Your Voice Agent Talking

Welcome to **10 Days of Voice Agents — #VoiceForBharat Edition**.

For Day 1, your objective is to:

- **Step 1:** Fork the repo. **Get the [starter repo](https://github.com/murf-ai/murf-livekit-starter) running** by following the setup and run instructions in its README, then add your API keys to the environment file.

  Alternatively

  - Build a voice agent in your favourite language and framework. **Using Murf Falcon for speech is the only hard requirement.**

- **Step 2: Pick your track** and build for it for all ten days. You can't switch after Day 3, because the work stacks.

  **Farm & Field** · **Health Access** · **Learning & Literacy** · **Local Commerce** · **Financial Services** · **Disaster Response**

- **Step 3: Give your agent an Indian voice.** Your agent has to speak in a Murf voice built for India — Indian English, or one of Hindi, Tamil, Bengali, Marathi, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Punjabi, Gujarati, Assamese or Odia.

- **Step 4: Successfully connect to your agent** and have a brief conversation with it. It doesn't need to be smart yet — it needs to hear you and talk back.

- **Step 5: Record a short video** of your session talking with the agent. Say your track out loud in the recording.

- **Step 6: Post the video on LinkedIn** with a description of what you built and the problem you're solving. Mention that you're building a voice agent using the fastest TTS API — **Murf Falcon**. Mention that you're part of **10 Days of Voice Agents - VoiceForBharat Edition** and don't forget to tag the official **Murf AI** handle. Also use the hashtag **#VoiceForBharat**.

- **Step 7: Submit your post link** on the submission form shared on Discord, along with your name and email. Do this every day — it's how your participation gets tracked.

Once your agent is running, your LinkedIn post is live and your form submission is in, you've completed Day 1.

Make sure your repo is public. Everything you build over the ten days lives there.

## Advanced (Optional)

You only need the steps above to complete Day 1. These are for going the extra mile:

- Justify your voice choice in one line — a children's tutor and a flood alert line wouldn't sound the same, even in the same language.
- Log the latency from end-of-user-speech to first audio out. Write the number down; you'll be optimising against it all challenge.
